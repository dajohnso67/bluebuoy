01

Executive Summary & Risk Analysis

FileMaker handles Blue Buoy's routine cases and hands the rest to staff. The migration's substance is therefore not porting features. It is absorbing the manual layer — month-end hand-corrections, an entire institutional billing business that never touched the database, and meaning encoded in typography — into a system that carries it.

All three bottlenecks are discovery or verification problems. None is a construction problem.

Bottleneck 01

Fog: institutional billing has no digital trace

Fourteen charter schools and nine Regional Center agencies exist in FileMaker as a text note telling staff how to bill. Everything downstream happens in QuickBooks, Excel, email, and paper. The fog has since partially lifted: the billing team has confirmed the workflow — rates negotiated each July, deliberately above the auto-pay rate; families obtain their own funding and purchase orders, with no authorization balance visible to Blue Buoy; month-end invoicing by email/paper or through each school's own portal; payment a month or more later; no supporting documentation required to release payment; and the entire receivables trail living as free-text notes (JAN 11237 $368, CK 7499). Still dark: the two portal submission workflows, the QuickBooks handoff, and how the receivables trail gets structured.

Move

The discovery track narrows to what's still dark — one real charter invoice, the tracking spreadsheet or QuickBooks view, and a walkthrough of each portal. The service log and authorization letter drop off the artifact list: both are now confirmed not to exist. The payer subsystem is decided — ADR-0002, no authorization balance tracking — and the Institutional payers phase is gated on the remaining artifacts.

Bottleneck 02

Money correctness has no clean oracle

Today's month-end run is manual posting, manual proration, a spreadsheet cross-reference against the card processor, manual referral-credit resets, and a void-or-refund window — now confirmed at 6–7 hours in a normal month and 10–12 across two days in summer, roughly 100 hours a year, with card mismatches every month. Because the outputs are the product of human correction, “match FileMaker” is not a trustworthy target: some of what it produced was wrong and staff caught it downstream — or didn't, as with a missed referral reset that silently keeps discounting a family.

Move

Reconcile by shadow run. Price closed months in the new system, compare per household to the cent, and classify every difference as a bug to fix or a FileMaker error to record. The run is green when no unexplained difference remains. Green months, not feature completion, are what authorize cutover.

The avoidable half of this risk is the card vault. Cards on file live with Authorize.Net; keeping that gateway carries them across intact. Changing gateways at cutover forces re-collecting every card from every family — the most damaging self-inflicted wound available to this project.

Bottleneck 03

Capability regression in search and encoded meaning

Deck Manager's ad-hoc find and its Saved Finds are the staff's power tool, and the questionnaire names the failure directly: replacing a capable search with a prettier, weaker one is how this kind of project goes wrong. Alongside it, real operational data lives in typography — ALL CAPS first names, colour highlights, note shorthand like AUG PO and MU. A straight import reads those as ordinary strings and the meaning evaporates silently, with nothing left to alert anyone.

Move

Make search parity an acceptance gate in the Scheduling & search phase, measured against the daily searches and Saved Finds that staff answers enumerate. Run a decode pass before import that converts each convention into typed data, and treat any convention still undecoded at import as a blocking defect.

Concurrent locking is a solved case, not a bottleneck

FileMaker's record locking becomes ordinary Postgres transactions plus two named invariants: an exclusion constraint making instructor double-booking impossible to commit, and a period-unique billing run making a double-charge impossible to commit. The database enforces both, rather than discipline, which is why neither earns a risk entry.

Core schema

The brief's four entities map onto Blue Buoy as: Customers → household and person; Schedules/Appointments → slot, enrollment, and lesson; Line Items → invoice_line; Invoices → invoice.

Identity
  • householdThe billing family unit. Siblings share one.
  • personOne row per human — name, contact, date of birth.
  • household_memberRole: student, guardian, billing contact, plus the relationship label FileMaker already stores. An adult student holds two roles rather than occupying two records.
  • student_profileLevel, status, pool, and an instructor-confirmed big-pool readiness date. Swim-specific attributes only; Level-4 students are surfaced to assess, never moved automatically.
  • instructorEmployment status.
Catalog & pricing
  • class_type · levelLookup tables replacing value lists.
  • eligibility_ruleAge and level bars as data, per class type or per slot — the deck screen bands Group classes individually (lv 8/9/10 beside lv 9-10). The group bars are confirmed: Parent & Me 0–3, Group 7+ and level 9–12, Stroke Tech 10+ and level 10–12, Adult 16+.
  • eligibility_overrideReason and authorizer. Exceptions stay possible and visible.
  • priceEffective-dated, so the annual increase is a new row and history survives.
  • discount_stepThe ordered sibling ladder and its cap, as rows. Tier order stays staff-controlled: a funded child or a group lesson goes first so the dearer lessons take the discount.
  • household_discountThe 10% first-responder discount, stacking with sibling and prepay, with whether ID was verified.
  • credit_conversion_rateThe confirmed exchange table: 2 semi-private = 1 private, 2 group = 1 semi-private, 4 group = 1 private, every direction. Rates as data.
  • class_pack · class_pack_useA second billing mode on the enrollment (ADR-0004): a single class or a pack of 4 or 8, sold as an invoice at a snapshot price, consumed one lesson per attendance marked present, idempotent on the attendance row. The billing run skips pack-mode enrollments; an empty pack never gates the roll — it becomes a deficit exception. No-show, expiry and discount rules are rows awaiting staff.
Scheduling
  • slotInstructor, weekday, time, class type, capacity. The recurring place a family holds.
  • slot_overrideAn empty group slot lent for one day to other lesson types when a sub is needed — a dated action, never an edit to the slot.
  • seat_hold · instructor_blockTwo constructs, never one flag: a semi-private seat kept free beside a child who needs it, with a review date defaulting to four weeks; and a teacher's break or a day's blocked time. Both excluded from availability everywhere.
  • enrollmentA student's claim on a slot, with a billing mode: monthly tuition or class pack. The billable relationship.
  • enrollment_offerA seat held while the family pays, until 5 pm the following day; then the seat returns and the payment request is cancelled.
  • lessonOne dated occurrence, held as a time range.
  • attendanceStatus, who marked it, which device, and a client UUID that makes offline replay safe.
  • closure · closure_waiverDate range, scope (all, one pool, one weekday), reason, and whether it issues credit. Bulk issuance lists everyone affected; per student, a credit or a waiver that prorates tuition instead — the goodwill waiver once per household, recorded so the rule is enforceable.
  • waitlist_entryDesired class type and the availability the family stated.
  • makeup_offerOne or two options offered to one family at a time, each slot genuinely held for a short window — an hour or two, adjustable — then passed to the next candidate. Minimises held time rather than maximising choice.
Money
  • payerHousehold, charter school, or Regional Center agency. The 14 and the 9 become rows, not a note.
  • payer_assignmentPer student, so siblings on different payers is the normal case.
  • funding_referenceA charter PO or Regional Center contract: reference number and kind, not a balance (ADR-0002 — no cap to consume). Its absence for a billing period is what surfaces, as an exception and a coverage-view line.
  • payment_applicationOne institutional check settling several students' invoices is the normal case; invoices carry a submitted → paid lifecycle, so receivables aging is a query.
  • price_agreementThe rate this enrollment pays, its reason, and the dates it holds.
  • invoice · invoice_lineLine unit amounts are snapshots: reprinting a two-year-old invoice reproduces it exactly.
  • credit · credit_application · credit_eventOne denominated ledger — private, semi-private, group — in whole-lesson units (ADR-0003). Booking a make-up reserves a credit, the lesson consumes it, a school cancellation releases it; conversions, sibling transfers, reversals and expiries are events, and nothing is deleted. Expiry is a rule row. Adult absences issue no credit. A balance is a query, never a field.
  • payment · payment_request · billing_exemptionNo cap on payments per month; a trade is a payment with a dollar value and a note. A payment request is a hosted link sent by SMS with a computed amount, so office staff never see a card. A billing exemption is today's "do not bill", with a reason from a short list.
  • billing_run · exceptionThe queue that replaces the spreadsheet cross-reference.
Flags, notes, documents
  • flagAllergy, support need, swim diaper, account handling. Typed data replacing typography; the office-only account-handling flag carries a reason and renders the guardian's name in caps on office screens, as staff read it today.
  • noteExplicit audience: office, instructor, or both.
  • documentInvoice PDFs, intake forms. Container fields and the institutional paper trail land here.
  • audit_eventEvery override action and deletion: what, who, when, why. Discretion stays; it becomes explicable.

Two invariants the database enforces

-- An instructor cannot be committed to two overlapping lessons.
CREATE EXTENSION IF NOT EXISTS btree_gist;
ALTER TABLE lesson ADD CONSTRAINT lesson_instructor_no_overlap
  EXCLUDE USING gist (instructor_id WITH =, during WITH &&)
  WHERE (status <> 'cancelled');

-- A period can be committed exactly once.
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX billing_run_one_commit_per_period
  ON billing_run (period) WHERE mode = 'committed';

Three modelling decisions that retire manual work

Each replaces a recurring month-end task rather than reimplementing it.

TodayModelWhat stops happening
Prices rise in January, the system applies the new rate to prepaid families anyway, and staff back out the difference every month afterwards price_agreement holds the locked rate with its own dates; invoice_line snapshots what it resolved The monthly back-out, and the month somebody forgets
A referral credit is a state someone must remember to clear next month; a missed reset discounts a family indefinitely with nothing flagging it A credit row valid for exactly one period, consumed by application and expired by date The reset step, and the silent over-discount
Make-up credits accumulate invisibly — one student holds 28 unused private credits — and convert between types at rates staff carry in their heads, with sibling transfers noted by hand One denominated ledger with a conversion-rate table and transfer events; expiry a policy field The surprise, the arithmetic at redemption, the hand-written transfer note — and the unused-credit report comes free, valued in one denomination

The first row carries a finding the current questionnaire dropped. FileMaker materializes billing months ahead of time, and a price change never reaches the rows it already created — while months created after the change take the new rate even for a family holding a locked prepay rate. Staff therefore correct in both directions, for opposite reasons, and the two corrections look nothing alike from the desk.

The new model materializes nothing ahead of issue. A future month has no row; the billing run prices it from the enrollment and its price agreement when the period arrives, and the invoice line snapshots the result at issue. Both corrections disappear along with the rows that required them.

03

Logic & Script Migration Strategy

Calculations

Sort every FileMaker calculation by what it is for, because the three kinds land in different places:

  • Presentational — a display name, a formatted total. Computed in the Templ template; nothing persists.
  • Derived and cheap — age from date of birth, a line total. A generated column, or computed at read.
  • Monetary and decided — the rate this enrollment pays. Resolved once by the pricing service and persisted.

That last reclassification is the important one. FileMaker recomputes a price whenever anyone looks, which is precisely why a January price rise reaches families who locked last year's rate. A resolved price is a fact about an agreement, not a lookup.

Script triggers become domain events

Each trigger becomes a named event the service publishes and handlers subscribe to, so one action can drive several consequences without any of them living inside a UI layout.

EventHandlers
enrollment.createdResolve price agreement, apply sibling step, check eligibility and capacity
enrollment.changedClose the outgoing price agreement, open the incoming one, mark the period for proration
lesson.cancelledIssue a make-up credit where policy calls for one, notify the family
closure.declaredCancel every lesson in range (optionally one pool or weekday), list everyone affected, issue credits or record a waiver and prorate, send the bulk notice
attendance.markedAdvance progression signals, feed the coverage view, consume a class-pack lesson for a pack-mode enrollment marked present (or record a deficit)
invoice.issuedRender the PDF, attach it to the payer, start the terms clock
payment.failedRaise an exception, notify the office, flag the card
makeup.booked · makeup.cancelledReserve the credit (converting first if staff chose to) / release it
credit.converted · credit.transferredConsume the source credits, issue the target at the table rate or to the sibling, record the actor
makeup.offered · offer.expiredHold the slot and reserve the credit for the window; on expiry release both and advance to the next candidate
enrollment.offered · enrollment_offer.expiredHold the seat and send the payment request; unpaid at 5 pm the next day, release the seat and cancel the request
payment_request.paidPost the payment, apply it, confirm the enrollment — no hand matching

Scheduled server scripts become cron jobs

Each holds a Postgres advisory lock and is idempotent on its period key, so a retry, an overlap, or two instances racing produce one outcome.

JobCadenceDoes
Billing runMonthlyPrices active enrollments, raises exceptions, issues invoices on commit
Credit expiry sweepNightlyExpires credits past their validity — never a reserved one — as an event; this is also what retires the manual referral reset
Card expiry noticeWeeklySurfaces cards on file about to lapse, ahead of a failed charge
Lesson materializationNightlyExtends lesson rows from slot definitions across the rolling window
Progression signalsWeeklyFeeds the attrition and level-progression reports, all of them queries over the model above — including the two-weeks-absent-without-notice list staff asked for
Enrollment snapshotWeeklyCounts active enrollments by class type for the week, so the year-over-year comparison staff compile by hand into Excel every day is a query
Prepay-ending and batch checkMonthlyLists prepays ending with enrollment continuing, and each auto-pay student's tuition against the pending Authorize.Net batch — the two monthly searches staff run by hand, surfaced as exceptions

The billing run is a state machine, not a script

draft→ priced→ exceptions cleared→ committed

It prices every active enrollment, and any household it cannot price cleanly becomes an exception rather than a silent guess: an unresolved mid-month change, a prepaid family whose lock expired, a card that failed last period, an institutional student with no funding reference for the period. Staff clear the queue; commit is available once it is empty. The same machine runs in shadow mode against closed months, producing a comparison instead of invoices.

The month-end work becomes clearing a short exception list rather than performing the whole run by hand.

Rules live as data

Eligibility bars, sibling discount steps, the first-responder percentage, prepay tiers, make-up conversion rates, make-up expiry and the legacy Christmas redemption boundary, closure credit policy, cancellation cutoffs, and the class-pack rules (no-shows, expiry, discounts, which types sell packs) are rows, not code. Staff answers arrive as configuration a staff member can change, and each carries an effective date, so changing a rule leaves last year's invoices reproducible.

API Contract & Integration Outline

Two surfaces, and no GraphQL

htmx wants HTML. The application's own screens are Templ fragments served straight from Chi handlers — no JSON round-trip, no client state to keep in sync. A separate, small JSON API serves the one client that genuinely needs data rather than markup: the pool-deck iPad taking attendance offline. GraphQL earns nothing here; it would add a schema layer for a single first-party consumer with a known access pattern.

Scheduling conflict check

One endpoint answers every “can this student take this slot” question — booking, waitlist matching, and drag-and-drop rescheduling all call it — so the rules have a single home.

POST /api/v1/schedule/check
{ "student_id": "...", "slot_id": "...", "starts_on": "2026-09-08" }

200 OK
{
  "bookable": false,
  "conflicts": [
    { "code": "instructor_busy",   "detail": "Overlaps 4:15pm private" },
    { "code": "capacity_full",     "detail": "3 of 3 enrolled" },
    { "code": "student_double",    "detail": "Already enrolled 4:00pm Tue" },
    { "code": "eligibility_unmet", "detail": "Group needs level 8, student at 6",
      "overridable": true },

Integrations

  • Authorize.Net — kept, with Customer Information Manager holding the card vault so cards survive cutover; Accept.js captures cards in the browser so numbers never touch the server; CIM charges a computed monthly amount, never ARB; the processor sits behind one internal interface. Enable Account Updater, which appears to be off. Volume is confirmed at $156k–$207k a month, which is why the stack stays. Payment requests ride the same gateway: a hosted link per household sent by SMS with a computed amount, so office staff never see a card; the product and flow are map tickets. The legacy card scrub is operations outside this plan, except that the frozen archive at cutover is made after it.
  • SMS — replaces fmSMS, which sits on a Twilio account; talk to Twilio directly and keep the sender number. Inbound replies need a destination.
  • QuickBooks — stays the accounting system; the app produces clean exports (the ledger, P&L and balance sheet the accountant receives each January) rather than replacing it.
  • PDF — server-side rendering of invoices, stored as documents.
  • Object storage — S3-compatible, holding what FileMaker container fields held plus institutional documentation.
05

Phased Execution Roadmap

Attendance-first, per ADR-0001 (2026-09-07): the December 2026 closure is the training and cutover window for attendance, and billing deliberately waits until one more annual rollover has run in FileMaker. Phases carry names, not numbers — the planning documents numbered theirs incompatibly. Each phase ends on a gate; passing a gate is an observation, not a judgement.

1

Foundations

The schema for identity, scheduling, and flags — money tables follow with Billing; the repeatable, non-destructive import for that slice; the decode pass narrowed to every deck-visible convention (ALL-CAPS student names, ALL-CAPS guardian names as the office-only handling flag, the under-4 diaper rule); individual accounts with PIN at shift start and session/device management; hosting, backup, and point-in-time recovery with one tested restore. An archive concept from the start — households restorable, never deleted — because the FM_2024 file exists only as a performance workaround, and what the import does with it is a map decision. The 2026-09-14 live export sized this phase: 5,654 students referenced by billing rows are missing from the live file, a third of schedule rows are holds, breaks or placeholders rather than enrollments, and instructor joins can go by id rather than name. The archaeology track — the remaining institutional artifacts — runs alongside without gating this phase.

Gate

The import runs from BlueBuoy_FM — confirmed live — and the BlueBuoy_FM_2024 archive is accounted for per the archive decision, including the students the live file's billing rows reference but no longer hold; household, student, enrollment, and flag counts reconcile against FileMaker; ten sampled households match field by field, including flags and notes; no deck-visible convention remains undecoded.

2

Attendance — target: December 2026 closure

The strict slice: roster view on all form factors (tablet-first), present/absent, backfill of a prior day, free instructor switching, profile-note indicator with the read-only two-channel display, diaper badge, offline caching of the full day's schedule for all instructors, PIN at shift start. Explicitly excluded: make-ups, scheduling changes, waitlist, billing visibility. Staff train during the two-week closure; the app is system of record at the January reopening.

Gate

A 2–4 week parallel run from the January reopening with attendance reconciled nightly against FileMaker — and instructors prefer it. If they don't, that gets fixed before any later phase proceeds.

3

Scheduling & search

Enrollment, schedule editing, waitlist, closures, and search. Search is the phase's centre of gravity, not a feature within it: a saved-search model plus a criteria builder reaching every field staff currently search, seeded with the Saved Finds they name. Plus make-up credit issuance and redemption — including conversion between denominations and sibling transfers, per ADR-0003 — closure handling with bulk issuance by weekday or pool and the once-per-household skip-and-prorate waiver, held seats and instructor blocks as two constructs, make-up offers of one or two options with short sequential holds, eligibility validation with override and per-class group bands, one-day repurposing of an empty group slot, the absence lookup as an instant action on the schedule board, the substitute-finding flow with its three constraints (instructor gender, level pairing, sibling proximity), and the deck-manager tools held out of the December slice.

The make-up ledger imports here by replay: every out-lesson becomes an issued credit, every booked make-up a consumption in the denomination it names, and the replayed balance is compared per student to FileMaker's stored counters — every difference a reconciliation exception, two causes already known. A rehearsal against the 2026-09-14 export found FileMaker's stored counters stale for most students with activity; which balance the import trusts is a map ticket.

Gate

Replayed make-up balances match FileMaker's stored counters per student, or every difference is classified; every search in the parity enumeration — the system's own scripted and structured searches plus the daily finds and Saved Finds staff name — returns the same set as FileMaker on the same data, and deck staff run one full week of real scheduling in the new UI alongside the old, including one full make-up cycle.

4

Billing

Begins only after the December 2026 rollover has run in FileMaker, and never cuts over in a peak month. The billing run state machine, pricing, credits, proration, rate-lock price agreements, the first-responder discount, enrollment holds and the one-year hold clock, class packs consumed on attendance (ADR-0004; legacy has nothing to replay, and the one hand-typed balance field turned out empty in the live export, so every adult lands on a review list with a balance to confirm), payment requests by hosted link so no staff member handles a card, the billing-exemption list with reasons, trade payments, the full transaction ledger, invoice PDFs, the Authorize.Net integration, and the monthly prepay-ending and tuition-vs-batch checks as exception views. Dry runs only; nothing charges a card until the shadow gate is green.

FileMaker's pre-created future billing months need a decision of their own before this phase's import runs. They are not history, they are pre-materialized future state, and some hold prices that went stale the day the price list changed. Import them as what they are — a queue of intentions — and they become enrollments the billing run will price when each period arrives.

Gate

Open-credit counts reconcile per denomination and every future-dated billing month is accounted for; a dry run of the last closed month reproduces every FileMaker invoice to the cent, or names the difference and its cause; then monthly shadow runs until three consecutive months land green.

5

Institutional payers

The payer subsystem per ADR-0002, built on the collected artifacts: per-payer configuration, funding references (a charter PO or an RC contract — never a tracked balance), month-end invoice generation, the no-funding-reference prompt, receivables aging, and structured invoice and check references replacing the free-text trail.

Gate

One full month invoiced through the system for every active institutional payer, with the receivables trail structured rather than free-text.

6

Cutover

Freeze FileMaker writes, run the final import, verify counts, point the gateway at the new system, and keep FileMaker readable for a year alongside a permanent frozen archive that includes BlueBuoy_FM_2024. The card vault stays where it is, so no family is asked for a card.

Rollback: available until the first committed billing run in the new system. Past that point, FileMaker no longer holds the month, and recovery is forward — which is why three green months, and not two, is the bar.
